# Research method and limits

## Research question

Which selected governance concepts can be compared across three international texts, and what additional evidence is needed before treating the approaches as interoperable?

## Coding method

Start with official sources and record their versions. Code a short paraphrase with a stable identifier and a paragraph/section locator. Assign one primary dimension per record. Link every analytical inference to evidence IDs and retain an unresolved question. An uncoded dimension is not evidence that a framework lacks it.

The source registry distinguishes a recommendation from political commitments. This is a research classification, not a legal opinion. The current sample excludes national/regional laws, enforcement and implementation outcomes. Add these layers before comparing binding duties.

## Source access

Sources were read through official webpages on 2026-11-24. The UN landing page and standalone PDF returned 403, so the adopted resolution text in the UN Mandate Registry was used instead. Annex I paragraphs 55(b), 55(d), 56(b) and 58 were checked. No negotiating draft was substituted. URLs are live pages, not archived snapshots; future revisions require a new version entry and review. Source documents are linked rather than redistributed.

## Quality controls

Eight automated negative/positive tests cover the evidence graph. They cannot certify semantic accuracy. All review states remain pending. A real reviewer should check context, the source's intended actors, normative force, omitted exceptions and whether the inference overreaches the cited material.

## AI assistance

Codex selected and paraphrased visible official source passages, drafted the synthesis, generated the validator and ran its tests. No runtime model integration or model evaluation study is claimed. The original research question and documents remain inspectable, and automated integrity checks do not count as independent human validation.
